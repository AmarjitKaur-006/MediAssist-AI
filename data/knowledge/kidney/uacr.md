# Urine Albumin-to-Creatinine Ratio (UACR)

## Test Name
Urine Albumin-to-Creatinine Ratio

## Aliases
UACR, ACR, Albumin-Creatinine Ratio

## Category
Kidney Function

## What Does This Test Measure?

UACR estimates the amount of albumin in urine relative to the amount of creatinine in the same urine sample.

Using a ratio helps account for how concentrated or diluted the urine sample is.

## Why Is This Test Performed?

UACR is commonly used to detect and monitor albuminuria, which can be a marker of kidney damage.

It is particularly important in people with diabetes, high blood pressure or other risk factors for kidney disease.

## What Can a Low Result Mean?

A lower UACR generally indicates that there is less albumin loss through the urine.

## What Can a High Result Mean?

A persistently elevated UACR may indicate kidney damage.

Higher levels can indicate greater degrees of albuminuria, but interpretation depends on the patient's clinical context and repeated measurements.

## Important Interpretation Factors

Temporary increases can occur with:

- Exercise
- Fever
- Infection
- Dehydration
- High blood sugar
- High blood pressure

UACR should therefore not be interpreted from one isolated abnormal sample whenever repeat testing is appropriate.

## Important Limitations

UACR is not a diagnosis by itself.

Persistence over time and other kidney measurements such as eGFR are important for evaluating chronic kidney disease.

## When Should the Result Be Discussed With a Healthcare Professional?

An abnormal or persistently elevated UACR should be discussed with a qualified healthcare professional.

## Related Tests / Context

Urine albumin, eGFR and creatinine.
