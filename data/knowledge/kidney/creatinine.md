# Creatinine

## Test Name
Creatinine

## Aliases
Serum Creatinine, Creat, SCr

## Category
Kidney Function

## What Does This Test Measure?

Creatinine is a waste product produced by normal muscle activity.

The kidneys normally filter creatinine from the blood and remove it through urine.

The creatinine blood test measures the amount of creatinine in the blood.

## Why Is This Test Performed?

Creatinine testing is commonly used to evaluate kidney function and is often considered together with eGFR and other kidney tests.

It may also be used to monitor known kidney disease or changes in kidney function.

## What Can a Low Result Mean?

Low blood creatinine may occur in people with lower muscle mass.

It can also be associated with conditions involving reduced muscle mass or severe malnutrition.

Low creatinine usually does not indicate impaired kidney filtration by itself.

## What Can a High Result Mean?

A high creatinine level may occur when the kidneys are not filtering creatinine effectively.

It can also be affected by:

- Dehydration
- Certain medicines
- High muscle mass
- Intense exercise
- Dietary factors

A high creatinine result does not by itself identify the cause of reduced kidney function.

## Important Interpretation Factors

Creatinine depends partly on muscle mass, age, sex and other factors.

It should be interpreted together with eGFR and other kidney-related measurements.

The laboratory's own reference range should be used.

## Important Limitations

Creatinine alone is not a perfect measure of kidney function.

A single abnormal value does not establish chronic kidney disease.

## When Should the Result Be Discussed With a Healthcare Professional?

An abnormal creatinine result should be reviewed by a qualified healthcare professional, especially if it is persistent or accompanied by other abnormal kidney findings.

## Related Tests / Context

eGFR, BUN, urine albumin and UACR.
