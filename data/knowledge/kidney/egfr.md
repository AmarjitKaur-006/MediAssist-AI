# Estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate (eGFR)

## Test Name
Estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate

## Aliases
eGFR, Estimated GFR

## Category
Kidney Function

## What Does This Test Measure?

eGFR estimates how well the kidneys are filtering blood.

It is usually calculated using blood creatinine together with demographic and other relevant factors used by the laboratory's equation.

## Why Is This Test Performed?

eGFR is widely used to evaluate kidney filtration and to help detect and monitor chronic kidney disease.

It is commonly interpreted together with urine albumin measurements.

## What Can a Low Result Mean?

A lower eGFR may indicate reduced kidney filtration.

A persistently reduced eGFR can be associated with chronic kidney disease.

The significance depends on the degree of reduction, persistence over time and other findings.

## What Can a High Result Mean?

An eGFR that is higher than expected is generally interpreted in the context of the person's overall health and the laboratory's reporting method.

There is usually greater clinical emphasis on reduced eGFR than on a high eGFR value.

## Important Interpretation Factors

eGFR is an estimate, not a direct measurement of filtration.

Interpretation may depend on:

- Age
- Sex
- Creatinine
- Body size and muscle mass
- Pregnancy
- Clinical context

Trends over time may be more informative than one isolated value.

## Important Limitations

eGFR equations are estimates and may be less reliable in certain situations.

A single low eGFR result does not automatically establish chronic kidney disease. Duration and other kidney markers are important.

## When Should the Result Be Discussed With a Healthcare Professional?

A reduced or unexpectedly abnormal eGFR should be discussed with a qualified healthcare professional, particularly when the finding persists or is accompanied by abnormal urine albumin results.

## Related Tests / Context

Creatinine, BUN, urine albumin and UACR.
