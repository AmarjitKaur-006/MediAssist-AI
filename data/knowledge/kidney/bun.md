# Blood Urea Nitrogen (BUN)

## Test Name
Blood Urea Nitrogen

## Aliases
BUN, Blood Urea Nitrogen

## Category
Kidney Function

## What Does This Test Measure?

BUN measures the amount of urea nitrogen in the blood.

Urea is a waste product produced when the body breaks down proteins. The kidneys normally remove urea from the blood.

## Why Is This Test Performed?

BUN testing may be used to:

- Evaluate kidney function
- Monitor kidney disease
- Help assess hydration
- Evaluate certain medical conditions affecting waste removal

## What Can a Low Result Mean?

Low BUN may occur with:

- Low protein intake
- Malnutrition
- Liver disease
- Overhydration

The result must be interpreted in context.

## What Can a High Result Mean?

High BUN may be associated with:

- Reduced kidney function
- Dehydration
- High protein intake
- Gastrointestinal bleeding
- Certain medicines or other medical conditions

A high BUN does not by itself prove kidney disease.

## Important Interpretation Factors

BUN should be interpreted together with creatinine, eGFR, hydration status and other clinical information.

## Important Limitations

BUN can be affected by factors other than kidney filtration, so it is not specific for kidney disease.

## When Should the Result Be Discussed With a Healthcare Professional?

An abnormal BUN result should be discussed with a qualified healthcare professional, particularly when it occurs with abnormal creatinine or eGFR.

## Related Tests / Context

Creatinine, eGFR, urine albumin and UACR.
