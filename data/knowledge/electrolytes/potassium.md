# Potassium

## Test Name
Potassium

## Aliases
K, Serum Potassium

## Category
Electrolytes

## What Does This Test Measure?

The potassium blood test measures the amount of potassium in the blood.

Potassium is an electrolyte that is important for normal nerve signals, muscle contraction and heart function.

## Why Is This Test Performed?

Potassium testing may be used to:

- Evaluate electrolyte balance
- Monitor kidney function
- Monitor certain medicines
- Evaluate conditions that may affect heart or muscle function

## What Can a Low Result Mean?

Low potassium is called hypokalemia.

Possible associations include:

- Vomiting or diarrhea
- Certain diuretics
- Low potassium intake
- Hormonal conditions
- Other medical conditions affecting potassium balance

## What Can a High Result Mean?

High potassium is called hyperkalemia.

Possible associations include:

- Reduced kidney function
- Certain medicines
- Tissue injury
- Hormonal conditions
- Certain metabolic disorders

High potassium can be clinically important because potassium affects heart rhythm.

## Important Interpretation Factors

Potassium results should be considered with kidney function, medications and other electrolyte measurements.

Hemolysis of a blood sample can sometimes falsely increase potassium.

## Important Limitations

A potassium result alone cannot determine the underlying cause of an abnormal value.

## When Should the Result Be Discussed With a Healthcare Professional?

Significantly abnormal potassium results should be discussed promptly with a qualified healthcare professional because major abnormalities can affect heart rhythm.

## Related Tests / Context

Sodium, chloride, bicarbonate, creatinine and BUN.
