# Magnesium

## Test Name
Magnesium

## Aliases
Serum Magnesium, Mg

## Category
Electrolytes / Mineral

## What Does This Test Measure?

The magnesium blood test measures the amount of magnesium in the blood.

Magnesium is important for normal nerve and muscle function, heart rhythm and many biochemical processes.

## Why Is This Test Performed?

Magnesium testing may be used to evaluate:

- Electrolyte abnormalities
- Muscle or heart-related symptoms
- Nutritional deficiency
- Kidney-related conditions
- Conditions affecting magnesium balance

## What Can a Low Result Mean?

Low magnesium is called hypomagnesemia.

It may occur with:

- Poor nutritional intake
- Gastrointestinal losses
- Certain medicines
- Alcohol-related conditions
- Kidney or metabolic disorders

Low magnesium can occur together with low potassium or calcium.

## What Can a High Result Mean?

High magnesium is called hypermagnesemia.

It is uncommon in people with normal kidney function and may be associated with:

- Reduced kidney function
- Excessive magnesium intake
- Certain medicines
- Other metabolic conditions

## Important Interpretation Factors

Magnesium should be interpreted together with kidney function and other electrolytes, particularly potassium and calcium.

## Important Limitations

A serum magnesium result may not fully represent total magnesium stores in the body.

## When Should the Result Be Discussed With a Healthcare Professional?

An abnormal magnesium result should be discussed with a qualified healthcare professional, particularly when significantly abnormal or associated with muscle, nerve or heart-related symptoms.

## Related Tests / Context

Calcium, potassium, sodium, creatinine and kidney-function tests.
