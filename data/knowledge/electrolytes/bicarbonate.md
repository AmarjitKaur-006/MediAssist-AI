# Bicarbonate

## Test Name
Bicarbonate

## Aliases
Bicarbonate, HCO3-, Total CO2, CO2

## Category
Electrolytes / Acid-Base Balance

## What Does This Test Measure?

The bicarbonate blood test measures the amount of bicarbonate in the blood.

Bicarbonate is an important part of the body's acid-base buffering system.

## Why Is This Test Performed?

Bicarbonate is commonly measured as part of an electrolyte panel or metabolic panel.

It may help evaluate:

- Acid-base balance
- Kidney function
- Certain metabolic disorders
- Conditions affecting breathing or body chemistry

## What Can a Low Result Mean?

Low bicarbonate may occur with:

- Metabolic acidosis
- Severe diarrhea
- Certain kidney disorders
- Certain metabolic conditions
- Other disturbances in acid-base balance

## What Can a High Result Mean?

High bicarbonate may occur with:

- Metabolic alkalosis
- Prolonged vomiting
- Some lung conditions
- Certain metabolic or kidney conditions

## Important Interpretation Factors

Bicarbonate should be interpreted together with sodium, chloride, kidney function and the broader clinical context.

## Important Limitations

An abnormal bicarbonate result does not identify the cause of an acid-base disturbance by itself.

## When Should the Result Be Discussed With a Healthcare Professional?

Discuss abnormal bicarbonate levels with a qualified healthcare professional, especially when accompanied by significant illness or other electrolyte abnormalities.

## Related Tests / Context

Sodium, chloride, potassium, creatinine, BUN and blood-gas testing when clinically indicated.
