# Sodium

## Test Name
Sodium

## Aliases
Na, Serum Sodium

## Category
Electrolytes

## What Does This Test Measure?

The sodium blood test measures the amount of sodium in the blood.

Sodium is an electrolyte that helps regulate fluid balance and supports normal nerve and muscle function.

## Why Is This Test Performed?

Sodium testing may be used to evaluate:

- Fluid and electrolyte balance
- Kidney function
- Conditions causing dehydration or fluid retention
- Certain hormone and metabolic disorders

It is commonly included in electrolyte panels and metabolic panels.

## What Can a Low Result Mean?

Low blood sodium is called hyponatremia.

Possible associations include:

- Excessive fluid intake or retention
- Certain medicines
- Kidney conditions
- Heart conditions
- Liver conditions
- Hormonal disorders
- Severe vomiting or diarrhea

The underlying cause must be determined clinically.

## What Can a High Result Mean?

High blood sodium is called hypernatremia.

It may occur with:

- Water loss
- Dehydration
- Certain hormonal or kidney-related conditions
- Excess sodium relative to body water

## Important Interpretation Factors

Sodium should be interpreted together with hydration status, kidney function, medications and other electrolytes.

## Important Limitations

An abnormal sodium level does not identify the underlying cause by itself.

## When Should the Result Be Discussed With a Healthcare Professional?

Significantly abnormal sodium results should be discussed promptly with a qualified healthcare professional, particularly when confusion, weakness, severe thirst, vomiting or other concerning symptoms are present.

## Related Tests / Context

Potassium, chloride, bicarbonate, creatinine, BUN and glucose.
