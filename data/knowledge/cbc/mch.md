# Mean Corpuscular Hemoglobin (MCH)

## Test Name
Mean Corpuscular Hemoglobin

## Aliases
MCH

## Category
Complete Blood Count (CBC) - Red Blood Cell Indices

## What Does This Test Measure?

MCH measures the average amount of hemoglobin in a single red blood cell.

It is one of the red blood cell indices reported with a CBC.

## Why Is This Test Performed?

MCH is interpreted with other red blood cell measurements to help evaluate anemia and other blood disorders.

## What Can a Low Result Mean?

A lower-than-usual MCH means that individual red blood cells contain less hemoglobin on average.

It may be associated with iron deficiency anemia.

## What Can a High Result Mean?

A higher-than-usual MCH means that red blood cells contain more hemoglobin on average.

An increased MCH may occur in some forms of anemia, including anemia associated with vitamin B deficiency.

## Important Interpretation Factors

MCH should be considered together with MCV, MCHC, RDW, hemoglobin, hematocrit and RBC count.

The laboratory's own reference range should be used for classification.

## Important Limitations

MCH alone cannot diagnose anemia or determine its cause.

The meaning of an abnormal MCH depends on the complete blood count and other clinical information.

## When Should the Result Be Discussed With a Healthcare Professional?

Discuss persistent or abnormal MCH results with a healthcare professional, especially when other CBC results are also abnormal.

## Related Tests / Context

MCV, MCHC, RDW, hemoglobin, hematocrit and RBC count.
