# Mean Corpuscular Volume (MCV)

## Test Name
Mean Corpuscular Volume

## Aliases
MCV, Mean Cell Volume

## Category
Complete Blood Count (CBC) - Red Blood Cell Indices

## What Does This Test Measure?

MCV measures the average size of red blood cells.

It is one of the red blood cell indices included in a CBC.

## Why Is This Test Performed?

MCV is commonly used with other CBC measurements to help evaluate anemia and other blood disorders.

The size of red blood cells can provide useful information when evaluating different types of anemia.

## What Can a Low Result Mean?

A low MCV means that red blood cells are smaller than usual.

It may be associated with conditions such as iron deficiency anemia or thalassemia.

## What Can a High Result Mean?

A high MCV means that red blood cells are larger than usual.

It may be associated with some forms of anemia, vitamin B12 or folate deficiency, and liver disease.

## Important Interpretation Factors

MCV should generally be interpreted together with hemoglobin, hematocrit, RBC count and other red blood cell indices.

Other factors such as age, diet, medicines, menstrual status and medical conditions can affect results.

## Important Limitations

MCV alone cannot determine the cause of anemia or another blood abnormality.

An MCV within the laboratory reference range does not exclude every type of anemia.

## When Should the Result Be Discussed With a Healthcare Professional?

Discuss an abnormal MCV with a healthcare professional, especially when it occurs together with abnormal hemoglobin, hematocrit or other CBC findings.

## Related Tests / Context

Hemoglobin, hematocrit, RBC count, MCH, MCHC and RDW.
