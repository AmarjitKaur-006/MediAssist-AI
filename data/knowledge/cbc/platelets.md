# Platelet Count

## Test Name
Platelet Count

## Aliases
Platelets, PLT, PLT Count, Thrombocyte Count

## Category
Complete Blood Count (CBC)

## What Does This Test Measure?

A platelet count measures the number of platelets in the blood.

Platelets are small blood cells that are essential for blood clotting and help stop bleeding after an injury.

## Why Is This Test Performed?

A platelet count is commonly included in a CBC.

It may be used to evaluate conditions associated with excessive bleeding or abnormal clotting and to monitor certain medical conditions or treatments.

## What Can a Low Result Mean?

A low platelet count is called thrombocytopenia.

Low platelet levels may increase the tendency to bleed.

There are many possible causes, including conditions affecting platelet production, destruction or distribution.

## What Can a High Result Mean?

A high platelet count is called thrombocytosis.

A high platelet count can sometimes increase the tendency for abnormal blood clotting, although the significance depends on the underlying cause and clinical context.

## Important Interpretation Factors

Platelet results should be interpreted using the reference range supplied by the laboratory.

The result should be considered together with symptoms, medical history and other blood-test findings.

## Important Limitations

A platelet count does not by itself determine the cause of an abnormal result.

Platelet count and platelet function are different concepts. A person can have an abnormal platelet count without the result alone explaining how effectively platelets function.

## When Should the Result Be Discussed With a Healthcare Professional?

An abnormal platelet count should be discussed with a qualified healthcare professional, particularly when the result is significantly abnormal or associated with unusual bleeding, bruising or clotting concerns.

## Related Tests / Context

CBC, platelet function tests, hemoglobin, hematocrit and WBC count.
