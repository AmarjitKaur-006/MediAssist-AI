# Hematocrit

## Test Name
Hematocrit

## Aliases
HCT, Packed Cell Volume, PCV

## Category
Complete Blood Count (CBC)

## What Does This Test Measure?

The hematocrit test measures the percentage of whole blood that is made up of red blood cells.

Red blood cells carry oxygen from the lungs to the rest of the body.

## Why Is This Test Performed?

Hematocrit is commonly measured as part of a complete blood count (CBC).

It may be used to evaluate conditions that affect red blood cells, investigate symptoms such as weakness or fatigue, or monitor certain blood-related conditions.

## What Can a Low Result Mean?

A low hematocrit result may be associated with:

- Anemia
- Recent or chronic blood loss
- Conditions that reduce red blood cell production
- Pregnancy, where increased blood volume can lower the percentage of blood made up of red blood cells
- Certain diseases affecting blood-cell production

A low result does not by itself identify the underlying cause.

## What Can a High Result Mean?

A high hematocrit result may be associated with:

- Dehydration
- Increased red blood cell production
- Certain lung diseases
- Certain heart conditions
- Polycythemia

Living at high altitude can also result in a higher hematocrit because the body responds to lower oxygen availability.

## Important Interpretation Factors

Hematocrit reference ranges can vary according to factors such as:

- Age
- Sex
- Smoking
- Altitude
- Pregnancy
- Laboratory methodology

The result should be interpreted together with hemoglobin, RBC count and other CBC measurements.

## Important Limitations

An abnormal hematocrit does not automatically indicate a specific disease.

The test alone cannot determine why the result is high or low.

## When Should the Result Be Discussed With a Healthcare Professional?

An abnormal hematocrit result should be discussed with a qualified healthcare professional, especially when it is persistent, significantly outside the laboratory reference range, or accompanied by symptoms.

## Related Tests / Context

Hemoglobin, RBC count, MCV, MCH, MCHC and RDW.
