# White Blood Cell (WBC) Count

## Test Name
White Blood Cell Count

## Aliases
WBC, WBC Count, White Cell Count, Leukocyte Count

## Category
Complete Blood Count (CBC)

## What Does This Test Measure?

A white blood cell count measures the number of white blood cells in the blood.

White blood cells are part of the immune system and help the body fight infections and other diseases.

## Why Is This Test Performed?

A WBC count may be used to help evaluate or monitor conditions involving infection, inflammation, immune-system disorders, or disorders affecting blood-cell production.

It is commonly included as part of a CBC.

## What Can a Low Result Mean?

A low WBC count may be associated with:

- Certain infections
- Bone marrow disorders or damage
- Some cancers affecting the bone marrow
- Autoimmune disorders
- HIV
- Certain medicines or treatments

A low WBC count does not by itself determine the underlying cause.

## What Can a High Result Mean?

A high WBC count may be associated with:

- Infection
- Inflammation
- Allergic reactions
- Certain cancers
- Tissue injury
- Stress
- Smoking
- Some medicines
- Pregnancy

## Important Interpretation Factors

A total WBC count does not show how many of each type of white blood cell are present.

A blood differential can measure the amount or percentage of neutrophils, lymphocytes, monocytes, eosinophils and basophils.

Interpretation should consider symptoms, medical history and other blood tests.

## Important Limitations

A high or low WBC count cannot confirm a diagnosis by itself.

Additional testing, such as a blood differential or other investigations, may be needed.

## When Should the Result Be Discussed With a Healthcare Professional?

Discuss an abnormal WBC count with a healthcare professional, especially when it is significantly abnormal, persistent, or associated with symptoms such as fever, recurrent infections or unexplained illness.

## Related Tests / Context

CBC, blood differential, RBC count, hemoglobin and platelet count.
