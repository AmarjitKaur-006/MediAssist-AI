# Red Cell Distribution Width (RDW)

## Test Name
Red Cell Distribution Width

## Aliases
RDW, RDW-CV, RDW-SD

## Category
Complete Blood Count (CBC) - Red Blood Cell Indices

## What Does This Test Measure?

RDW measures how much the size and volume of red blood cells vary from one cell to another.

When red blood cells are similar in size, the variation is smaller. A higher RDW indicates greater variation in red blood cell size.

## Why Is This Test Performed?

RDW is commonly part of a CBC.

It can help healthcare professionals evaluate or classify anemia and may be interpreted alongside other red blood cell indices.

## What Can a Low Result Mean?

A low RDW generally indicates that red blood cells are relatively similar in size.

An isolated low RDW is generally not interpreted as a specific disease diagnosis.

## What Can a High Result Mean?

A high RDW indicates greater variation in red blood cell size.

It may occur in some types of anemia and can provide additional information when interpreted with other CBC measurements.

## Important Interpretation Factors

RDW should be interpreted together with tests such as MCV, MCH, MCHC, hemoglobin and hematocrit.

A high RDW is not specific to one disease.

## Important Limitations

RDW alone cannot diagnose a specific condition.

Healthcare professionals usually combine RDW with other blood-test results, symptoms and medical history.

## When Should the Result Be Discussed With a Healthcare Professional?

Discuss an abnormal RDW result with a healthcare professional, especially when it occurs together with abnormal hemoglobin, MCV or other CBC findings.

## Related Tests / Context

MCV, MCH, MCHC, hemoglobin, hematocrit and RBC count.
