# Hemoglobin

## Test Name
Hemoglobin

## Aliases
Hb, Hgb, H&H

## Category
Complete Blood Count (CBC)

## What Does This Test Measure?

The hemoglobin test measures the amount of hemoglobin in the blood.

Hemoglobin is an iron-rich protein found in red blood cells. It carries oxygen from the lungs to the rest of the body.

Hemoglobin is commonly measured as part of a Complete Blood Count (CBC).

## Why Is This Test Performed?

A hemoglobin test may be performed as part of a routine health examination or when a healthcare professional wants to evaluate symptoms or conditions that may affect red blood cells or oxygen delivery.

It may be used when there are symptoms associated with anemia, a family history of inherited blood disorders, possible nutritional deficiencies, long-term infection, or blood loss.

It can also be used to monitor anemia or other conditions that may affect hemoglobin levels.

## What Can a Low Result Mean?

A low hemoglobin result may be associated with:

- Different types of anemia
- Iron deficiency
- Thalassemia
- Blood loss
- Liver disease
- Certain cancers and other diseases
- Lack of nutrients such as iron or vitamin B12

A low hemoglobin result does not by itself establish the underlying cause.

## What Can a High Result Mean?

A high hemoglobin result may be associated with:

- Certain lung diseases
- Certain heart diseases
- Polycythemia vera
- Sleep apnea

Higher hemoglobin can also occur for reasons such as living at a high altitude or dehydration.

## Important Interpretation Factors

Hemoglobin results should be interpreted using the reference range provided by the laboratory.

Reference ranges can vary between laboratories and may also be affected by factors such as age and sex.

Other factors that may affect hemoglobin results include:

- Diet
- Physical activity
- Certain medicines
- Dehydration
- Smoking
- Certain genetic conditions
- Living at higher altitude

The result should be considered together with symptoms, medical history, and other blood-test results.

## Important Limitations

A hemoglobin result outside the laboratory reference range does not automatically mean that a person has a specific disease.

The hemoglobin test cannot by itself determine the cause of an abnormal result.

A healthcare professional may need to consider other CBC measurements, medical history, symptoms, and additional laboratory tests.

MediAssist AI should therefore provide an informational explanation rather than a diagnosis.

## When Should the Result Be Discussed With a Healthcare Professional?

A person should discuss an abnormal hemoglobin result or concerns about the result with a qualified healthcare professional.

Professional interpretation is particularly important when the result is significantly abnormal, persistent, or accompanied by symptoms.


## Related Tests / Context

Hemoglobin is commonly interpreted alongside other Complete Blood Count (CBC) measurements such as hematocrit, RBC count, MCV, MCH, MCHC, and RDW.

Depending on the clinical context, additional tests such as ferritin, iron studies, vitamin B12, and folate may also be considered.
