# Red Blood Cell (RBC) Count

## Test Name
Red Blood Cell Count

## Aliases
RBC, RBC Count, Red Cell Count, Erythrocyte Count

## Category
Complete Blood Count (CBC)

## What Does This Test Measure?

An RBC count measures the number of red blood cells in the blood.

Red blood cells contain hemoglobin and carry oxygen from the lungs to cells throughout the body.

## Why Is This Test Performed?

RBC count is usually part of a complete blood count (CBC).

It may help healthcare professionals evaluate blood disorders and conditions that affect the number of red blood cells.

## What Can a Low Result Mean?

A low RBC count may be associated with conditions that reduce the number of circulating red blood cells, including certain types of anemia or blood loss.

The cause cannot be determined from the RBC count alone.

## What Can a High Result Mean?

A high RBC count may be associated with increased red blood cell production or reduced plasma volume.

Possible contributing conditions or circumstances can include dehydration, some heart or lung conditions, and living at high altitude.

## Important Interpretation Factors

RBC results should be interpreted using the laboratory's reference range.

Interpretation also depends on factors such as:

- Age
- Sex
- Altitude
- Smoking
- Hydration
- Other CBC findings

RBC count is commonly interpreted together with hemoglobin, hematocrit and RBC indices.

## Important Limitations

An abnormal RBC count does not by itself establish a diagnosis.

Additional blood tests and clinical information may be needed to determine the cause.

## When Should the Result Be Discussed With a Healthcare Professional?

Discuss an abnormal RBC count with a qualified healthcare professional, particularly when the result is persistent or associated with symptoms.

## Related Tests / Context

Hemoglobin, hematocrit, MCV, MCH, MCHC and RDW.
