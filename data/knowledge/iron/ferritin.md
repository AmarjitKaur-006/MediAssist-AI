# Ferritin

## Test Name
Ferritin

## Aliases
Ferritin Blood Test, Serum Ferritin

## Category
Iron Studies

## What Does This Test Measure?

Ferritin is a protein that stores iron in the body.

A ferritin blood test measures the amount of ferritin in the blood and is commonly used as an indicator of the body's stored iron.

## Why Is This Test Performed?

Ferritin testing may be used to:

- Evaluate possible iron deficiency
- Investigate some forms of anemia
- Evaluate possible iron overload
- Monitor iron-related treatment

## What Can a Low Result Mean?

Low ferritin generally suggests that the body's iron stores are reduced.

It may occur with iron deficiency, blood loss or inadequate iron intake or absorption.

## What Can a High Result Mean?

High ferritin may occur with:

- Iron overload
- Inflammation
- Infection
- Liver disease
- Certain cancers
- Metabolic conditions

High ferritin does not necessarily mean that the body has excess stored iron.

## Important Interpretation Factors

Ferritin can increase during inflammation or illness because it is also an acute-phase reactant.

It should therefore be considered together with other iron studies and clinical findings.

## Important Limitations

Ferritin alone cannot distinguish iron overload from all causes of elevated ferritin.

## When Should the Result Be Discussed With a Healthcare Professional?

An abnormal ferritin result should be reviewed with a qualified healthcare professional, especially when persistent or accompanied by abnormal iron studies.

## Related Tests / Context

Serum iron, TIBC, transferrin and transferrin saturation.
