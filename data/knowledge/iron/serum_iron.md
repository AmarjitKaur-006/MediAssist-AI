# Serum Iron

## Test Name
Serum Iron

## Aliases
Serum Iron, Iron Blood Test

## Category
Iron Studies

## What Does This Test Measure?

The serum iron test measures the amount of iron circulating in the blood.

Iron is needed for hemoglobin production and several other biological processes.

## Why Is This Test Performed?

Serum iron may be measured as part of an iron study to help evaluate possible iron deficiency, iron overload, or certain forms of anemia.

## What Can a Low Result Mean?

Low serum iron may be associated with:

- Iron deficiency
- Blood loss
- Chronic disease or inflammation
- Reduced iron absorption
- Inadequate iron intake

## What Can a High Result Mean?

High serum iron may occur with:

- Excess iron intake or supplementation
- Iron overload disorders
- Certain liver conditions
- Increased red blood cell breakdown
- Other medical conditions

## Important Interpretation Factors

Serum iron can vary during the day and can be affected by food intake, medicines and other factors.

It should generally be interpreted together with ferritin, TIBC and transferrin saturation.

## Important Limitations

Serum iron alone cannot diagnose iron deficiency or iron overload.

## When Should the Result Be Discussed With a Healthcare Professional?

An abnormal serum iron result should be discussed with a qualified healthcare professional, particularly when other iron studies are also abnormal.

## Related Tests / Context

Ferritin, TIBC, transferrin and transferrin saturation.
