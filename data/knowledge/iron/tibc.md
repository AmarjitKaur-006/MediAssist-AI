# Total Iron-Binding Capacity (TIBC)

## Test Name
Total Iron-Binding Capacity

## Aliases
TIBC, Total Iron Binding Capacity

## Category
Iron Studies

## What Does This Test Measure?

TIBC measures the blood's capacity to bind and transport iron.

It is commonly interpreted as part of an iron study.

## Why Is This Test Performed?

TIBC may help evaluate possible:

- Iron deficiency
- Iron overload
- Anemia
- Disorders of iron metabolism

## What Can a Low Result Mean?

A low TIBC may occur with:

- Chronic disease or inflammation
- Malnutrition
- Liver disease
- Iron overload
- Other conditions affecting protein production

## What Can a High Result Mean?

High TIBC is often associated with iron deficiency.

The result should be interpreted together with ferritin and serum iron.

## Important Interpretation Factors

TIBC is commonly interpreted with serum iron, ferritin, transferrin and transferrin saturation.

## Important Limitations

TIBC alone cannot determine the cause of an abnormal iron status.

## When Should the Result Be Discussed With a Healthcare Professional?

An abnormal TIBC result should be discussed with a qualified healthcare professional, particularly when other iron studies are abnormal.

## Related Tests / Context

Serum iron, ferritin, transferrin and transferrin saturation.
