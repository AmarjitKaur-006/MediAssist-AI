# C-Reactive Protein (CRP)

## Test Name
C-Reactive Protein

## Aliases
CRP, C-Reactive Protein

## Category
Inflammation

## What Does This Test Measure?

C-reactive protein is a protein produced by the liver in response to inflammation.

The CRP blood test measures the amount of CRP in the blood.

## Why Is This Test Performed?

CRP testing may be used to help detect or monitor inflammation associated with infections, inflammatory conditions and other medical conditions.

It may also be used to monitor changes in inflammation over time.

## What Can a Low Result Mean?

Low CRP levels generally indicate little measurable systemic inflammation at the time of testing.

## What Can a High Result Mean?

A high CRP level indicates that inflammation may be present.

Possible associations include:

- Infection
- Autoimmune or inflammatory conditions
- Tissue injury
- Certain cancers
- Other conditions causing inflammation

The result does not identify the cause by itself.

## Important Interpretation Factors

CRP is a nonspecific marker.

It should be interpreted with symptoms, medical history and other laboratory or diagnostic findings.

## Important Limitations

CRP cannot determine the location or exact cause of inflammation by itself.

An elevated CRP does not automatically mean that an infection is present.

## When Should the Result Be Discussed With a Healthcare Professional?

An abnormal CRP result should be discussed with a qualified healthcare professional, especially when persistent or accompanied by symptoms.

## Related Tests / Context

ESR, CBC and other investigations based on the clinical situation.
