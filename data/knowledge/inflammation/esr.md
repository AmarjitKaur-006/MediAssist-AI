# Erythrocyte Sedimentation Rate (ESR)

## Test Name
Erythrocyte Sedimentation Rate

## Aliases
ESR, Sed Rate, Sedimentation Rate

## Category
Inflammation

## What Does This Test Measure?

ESR measures how quickly red blood cells settle to the bottom of a test tube over a specified period.

The rate can be influenced by proteins associated with inflammation.

## Why Is This Test Performed?

ESR may be used to help detect inflammation and to monitor certain inflammatory conditions.

It is often interpreted alongside symptoms and other laboratory tests.

## What Can a Low Result Mean?

A low ESR may occur when there is little measurable inflammation, but the result is not normally interpreted by itself.

## What Can a High Result Mean?

A high ESR may indicate inflammation.

Possible associations include:

- Infections
- Autoimmune diseases
- Other inflammatory conditions
- Certain cancers
- Some blood-related conditions

An elevated ESR does not identify the cause of inflammation.

## Important Interpretation Factors

ESR can be affected by factors other than inflammation, including age, sex, pregnancy, anemia and some other conditions.

It should therefore be interpreted with other findings.

## Important Limitations

ESR is nonspecific.

A high or low ESR cannot by itself diagnose a specific disease.

## When Should the Result Be Discussed With a Healthcare Professional?

Discuss an abnormal ESR result with a qualified healthcare professional, particularly when persistent or accompanied by symptoms.

## Related Tests / Context

CRP, CBC and other inflammation-related investigations.
