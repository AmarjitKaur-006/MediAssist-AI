# Direct Bilirubin

## Test Name
Direct Bilirubin

## Aliases
Direct Bilirubin, Conjugated Bilirubin

## Category
Liver Function

## What Does This Test Measure?

Direct bilirubin refers mainly to bilirubin that has been processed by the liver and made water-soluble.

The test measures the amount of direct or conjugated bilirubin in the blood.

## Why Is This Test Performed?

Direct bilirubin may be measured together with total and indirect bilirubin to help evaluate:

- Liver disease
- Bile-duct obstruction
- Jaundice
- Disorders affecting bilirubin metabolism

## What Can a Low Result Mean?

Low direct bilirubin levels are generally not considered clinically concerning by themselves.

## What Can a High Result Mean?

High direct bilirubin may occur when processed bilirubin cannot be transported normally through the bile system.

Possible associations include liver disease and blockage or impairment of bile flow.

The result must be interpreted with total bilirubin, indirect bilirubin and other clinical information.

## Important Interpretation Factors

Direct bilirubin is normally interpreted together with total bilirubin and, when available, indirect bilirubin.

Laboratory reference ranges may differ.

## Important Limitations

Direct bilirubin alone cannot determine the underlying cause of an abnormal result.

Further laboratory testing and clinical assessment may be necessary.

## When Should the Result Be Discussed With a Healthcare Professional?

An abnormal direct bilirubin result should be discussed with a qualified healthcare professional, particularly if jaundice or other liver-related symptoms are present.

## Related Tests / Context

Total bilirubin, indirect bilirubin, ALT, AST, ALP and GGT.
