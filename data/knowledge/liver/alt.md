# Alanine Aminotransferase (ALT)

## Test Name
Alanine Aminotransferase

## Aliases
ALT, SGPT, GPT, Alanine Transaminase

## Category
Liver Function

## What Does This Test Measure?

ALT is an enzyme found mainly in the liver.

The test measures the amount of ALT in the blood. When liver cells are damaged, ALT can be released into the bloodstream.

## Why Is This Test Performed?

ALT is commonly measured as part of a group of liver tests.

It may be used to:

- Evaluate possible liver injury or disease
- Monitor an existing liver condition
- Investigate symptoms such as fatigue, nausea, abdominal discomfort or jaundice
- Monitor the effects of certain medicines or treatments

## What Can a Low Result Mean?

Low ALT levels are generally not a major concern.

Unusually low levels may sometimes be associated with conditions such as vitamin B6 deficiency or chronic kidney disease.

## What Can a High Result Mean?

Higher-than-usual ALT levels may be associated with liver injury or disease, including:

- Hepatitis
- Cirrhosis
- Liver infections
- Liver cancer
- Other liver diseases
- Reduced blood flow to the liver
- Certain medicines or substances

An elevated ALT result does not by itself establish a specific diagnosis.

## Important Interpretation Factors

ALT should be interpreted together with other liver tests and the laboratory's own reference range.

Factors such as age, sex, weight, medicines, supplements, intense exercise and other health conditions may affect ALT levels.

## Important Limitations

ALT is not specific enough to identify the cause of liver injury by itself.

A high result does not automatically mean that a person has a condition requiring treatment.

## When Should the Result Be Discussed With a Healthcare Professional?

An abnormal ALT result should be reviewed with a qualified healthcare professional, especially when it is persistent, significantly abnormal or accompanied by symptoms.

## Related Tests / Context

AST, ALP, GGT, bilirubin, albumin and total protein.
