# Aspartate Aminotransferase (AST)

## Test Name
Aspartate Aminotransferase

## Aliases
AST, SGOT, GOT, Aspartate Transaminase

## Category
Liver Function

## What Does This Test Measure?

AST is an enzyme found in the liver as well as in the heart, muscles and other tissues.

The test measures the amount of AST in the blood. Damaged cells can release AST into the bloodstream.

## Why Is This Test Performed?

AST is commonly used with other liver tests to evaluate liver health.

It may be used to investigate possible liver disease, monitor a known liver condition, or evaluate symptoms associated with liver injury.

## What Can a Low Result Mean?

Low AST levels are usually considered normal and are generally not a concern.

## What Can a High Result Mean?

High AST levels may be associated with liver disease or other conditions that damage tissues containing AST.

Possible associations include:

- Hepatitis
- Cirrhosis
- Mononucleosis
- Heart conditions
- Pancreatitis
- Hemochromatosis
- Muscle or tissue injury
- Certain medicines or substances

## Important Interpretation Factors

AST is not specific to the liver because it is also found in muscles, heart and other tissues.

It should therefore be considered together with ALT and other liver tests.

Pregnancy, exercise, medicines, age and sex can also affect results.

## Important Limitations

An elevated AST does not establish the cause or location of tissue injury by itself.

AST cannot distinguish liver injury from injury involving other tissues without additional information.

## When Should the Result Be Discussed With a Healthcare Professional?

Discuss an abnormal AST result with a qualified healthcare professional, particularly when it is persistent or accompanied by symptoms or other abnormal liver tests.

## Related Tests / Context

ALT, ALP, GGT, bilirubin, albumin and total protein.
