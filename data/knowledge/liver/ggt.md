# Gamma-Glutamyl Transferase (GGT)

## Test Name
Gamma-Glutamyl Transferase

## Aliases
GGT, GGTP, Gamma-GT, GTP, Gamma-Glutamyl Transpeptidase

## Category
Liver Function

## What Does This Test Measure?

GGT is an enzyme found throughout the body but mainly in the liver.

The test measures the amount of GGT in the blood.

## Why Is This Test Performed?

GGT may be used to:

- Help evaluate liver disease
- Evaluate possible bile-duct problems
- Help interpret a high ALP result
- Monitor some liver-related conditions
- Monitor alcohol-related treatment in appropriate clinical settings

## What Can a Low Result Mean?

Low or normal GGT levels generally do not suggest liver disease by themselves.

## What Can a High Result Mean?

High GGT levels may be associated with:

- Liver disease
- Bile-duct damage or obstruction
- Hepatitis
- Cirrhosis
- Alcohol use
- Certain medicines
- Pancreatitis
- Some other medical conditions

## Important Interpretation Factors

GGT should generally be interpreted with ALP and other liver tests.

High ALP together with high GGT can support a liver or bile-duct source for the abnormality, while high ALP with normal or low GGT may make a bone source more likely.

## Important Limitations

GGT cannot identify the specific cause of liver damage by itself.

Alcohol and certain medicines can affect GGT.

## When Should the Result Be Discussed With a Healthcare Professional?

Abnormal GGT results should be discussed with a qualified healthcare professional, particularly when combined with abnormal ALP or other liver tests.

## Related Tests / Context

ALP, ALT, AST, bilirubin and other liver-function tests.
