# Total Bilirubin

## Test Name
Total Bilirubin

## Aliases
Total Bilirubin, TSB, TBIL, Total Serum Bilirubin

## Category
Liver Function

## What Does This Test Measure?

Bilirubin is a yellowish substance produced when the body breaks down old red blood cells.

The liver processes bilirubin and removes it from the body through bile.

A total bilirubin test measures the overall amount of bilirubin in the blood.

## Why Is This Test Performed?

Bilirubin testing may be used to:

- Evaluate liver health
- Investigate jaundice
- Look for bile-duct obstruction
- Monitor liver disease
- Evaluate conditions involving increased red blood cell breakdown

## What Can a Low Result Mean?

Low bilirubin levels are generally not considered a significant health concern.

## What Can a High Result Mean?

High bilirubin levels may be associated with:

- Liver disease
- Bile-duct obstruction
- Hemolytic anemia
- Certain other conditions affecting bilirubin processing

High bilirubin can cause jaundice, which may appear as yellowing of the skin or eyes.

## Important Interpretation Factors

Bilirubin results should be interpreted using the laboratory's reference range.

Total bilirubin may be considered together with direct and indirect bilirubin and other liver tests.

Age, sex, medicines and the specific clinical context can affect interpretation.

## Important Limitations

An elevated bilirubin level does not by itself establish the cause of jaundice or liver dysfunction.

Additional tests may be needed.

## When Should the Result Be Discussed With a Healthcare Professional?

An abnormal bilirubin result should be reviewed by a qualified healthcare professional, particularly when accompanied by jaundice, dark urine, pale stools, abdominal symptoms or other abnormal liver tests.

## Related Tests / Context

Direct bilirubin, indirect bilirubin, ALT, AST, ALP, GGT and albumin.
