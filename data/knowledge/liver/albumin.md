# Albumin

## Test Name
Albumin

## Aliases
Serum Albumin, Albumin Blood Test

## Category
Liver Function / Blood Proteins

## What Does This Test Measure?

Albumin is a protein made by the liver.

It helps keep fluid inside blood vessels and also transports hormones, medicines, vitamins and other substances through the blood.

The albumin blood test measures the amount of albumin in the blood.

## Why Is This Test Performed?

Albumin may be measured to:

- Evaluate general health and nutrition
- Help assess liver function
- Help assess kidney function
- Monitor certain liver or kidney conditions
- Investigate abnormal protein levels

## What Can a Low Result Mean?

Low albumin may be associated with:

- Liver disease
- Kidney disease
- Malnutrition
- Severe burns
- Other conditions affecting protein levels

Low albumin does not automatically mean that liver disease is present.

## What Can a High Result Mean?

High albumin levels may be associated with dehydration.

High albumin is less common than low albumin and should be interpreted in context.

## Important Interpretation Factors

Albumin should be interpreted with other liver, kidney and protein-related tests.

Pregnancy and other physiological factors can affect albumin levels.

## Important Limitations

Albumin is not specific to liver disease.

Low albumin can occur for several different reasons, so the result alone cannot determine the cause.

## When Should the Result Be Discussed With a Healthcare Professional?

An abnormal albumin result should be discussed with a qualified healthcare professional, particularly if it is persistent or occurs with other abnormal liver, kidney or nutritional findings.

## Related Tests / Context

Total protein, globulins, A/G ratio, bilirubin, ALT, AST, creatinine and urine albumin.
