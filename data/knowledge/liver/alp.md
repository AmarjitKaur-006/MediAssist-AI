# Alkaline Phosphatase (ALP)

## Test Name
Alkaline Phosphatase

## Aliases
ALP, ALK, PHOS, ALKP, Alkaline Phosphatase

## Category
Liver Function

## What Does This Test Measure?

ALP is an enzyme found throughout the body, with higher amounts in the liver, bile ducts and bones.

The test measures the amount of ALP in the blood.

## Why Is This Test Performed?

ALP may be used to evaluate liver or bile-duct conditions and is also commonly used when evaluating bone disorders.

It may be part of a comprehensive metabolic panel or liver testing.

## What Can a Low Result Mean?

Low ALP levels are less common.

They may be associated with conditions such as:

- Zinc deficiency
- Protein deficiency
- Malnutrition
- Pernicious anemia
- Thyroid disease
- Wilson disease
- Hypophosphatasia

## What Can a High Result Mean?

High ALP levels may be associated with:

- Liver disease
- Bile-duct blockage
- Cirrhosis
- Hepatitis
- Certain bone disorders
- Some infections or other conditions

ALP may also be elevated during periods of bone growth.

## Important Interpretation Factors

ALP is produced by more than one tissue, particularly the liver and bones.

The ALP result should therefore be interpreted with other tests and clinical information.

A GGT test may sometimes be used alongside ALP to help determine whether a high ALP is more likely related to the liver or a bone source.

## Important Limitations

An ALP test alone cannot determine the source of an elevated ALP or establish a specific diagnosis.

## When Should the Result Be Discussed With a Healthcare Professional?

Discuss abnormal ALP results with a qualified healthcare professional, especially when the elevation is persistent or occurs with symptoms or other abnormal liver tests.

## Related Tests / Context

ALT, AST, GGT, bilirubin and other liver-function measurements.
