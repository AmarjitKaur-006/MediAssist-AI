# Total Protein

## Test Name
Total Protein

## Aliases
Total Serum Protein, TP, Total Protein Blood Test

## Category
Liver Function / Blood Proteins

## What Does This Test Measure?

The total protein test measures the total amount of protein in the blood.

The main protein groups measured are albumin and globulins.

## Why Is This Test Performed?

Total protein testing may be used as part of a comprehensive metabolic panel or to evaluate:

- Liver function
- Kidney function
- Nutritional status
- Conditions affecting blood proteins

## What Can a Low Result Mean?

Low total protein levels may be associated with:

- Liver disease
- Kidney disease
- Malnutrition
- Malabsorption conditions

## What Can a High Result Mean?

High total protein levels may be associated with:

- Certain infections
- Chronic inflammatory states
- Some blood disorders

The cause cannot be identified from total protein alone.

## Important Interpretation Factors

Total protein should be considered together with albumin, globulin and the albumin/globulin ratio when available.

The laboratory's own reference range should be used.

## Important Limitations

An abnormal total protein result is nonspecific.

It cannot by itself determine whether the cause is related to the liver, kidneys, nutrition, inflammation or another condition.

## When Should the Result Be Discussed With a Healthcare Professional?

An abnormal total protein result should be reviewed with a qualified healthcare professional, particularly when persistent or accompanied by other abnormal laboratory results or symptoms.

## Related Tests / Context

Albumin, globulin, A/G ratio, ALT, AST, bilirubin and kidney-function tests.
