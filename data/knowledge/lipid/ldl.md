# LDL Cholesterol

## Test Name
Low-Density Lipoprotein Cholesterol

## Aliases
LDL, LDL-C, Low-Density Lipoprotein

## Category
Lipid Profile

## What Does This Test Measure?

LDL cholesterol measures the amount of cholesterol carried in low-density lipoprotein particles in the blood.

LDL is commonly described as a type of cholesterol associated with cholesterol buildup in arteries.

## Why Is This Test Performed?

LDL cholesterol is commonly measured as part of a lipid profile to help assess cardiovascular risk and monitor treatment.

## What Can a Low Result Mean?

Lower LDL cholesterol is generally associated with lower cardiovascular risk.

An unusually low LDL result should still be interpreted in the context of the person's overall health and treatment.

## What Can a High Result Mean?

Higher LDL cholesterol is associated with increased risk of plaque buildup in arteries and cardiovascular disease.

## Important Interpretation Factors

LDL interpretation depends on the individual's overall cardiovascular risk.

Factors such as diabetes, previous cardiovascular disease, age, smoking and blood pressure may influence treatment goals.

## Important Limitations

LDL cholesterol alone cannot determine whether a person will develop cardiovascular disease.

Treatment targets vary according to individual risk.

## When Should the Result Be Discussed With a Healthcare Professional?

An elevated LDL result should be discussed with a qualified healthcare professional, especially when other cardiovascular risk factors are present.

## Related Tests / Context

Total cholesterol, HDL cholesterol, triglycerides and non-HDL cholesterol.
