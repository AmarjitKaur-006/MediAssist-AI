# HDL Cholesterol

## Test Name
High-Density Lipoprotein Cholesterol

## Aliases
HDL, HDL-C, High-Density Lipoprotein

## Category
Lipid Profile

## What Does This Test Measure?

HDL cholesterol measures cholesterol carried by high-density lipoprotein particles in the blood.

HDL is commonly referred to as a type of cholesterol associated with removing cholesterol from the bloodstream.

## Why Is This Test Performed?

HDL is measured as part of a lipid profile to help assess cardiovascular risk.

## What Can a Low Result Mean?

Lower HDL levels may be associated with increased cardiovascular risk, particularly when other risk factors are present.

## What Can a High Result Mean?

Higher HDL is often associated with lower cardiovascular risk.

However, very high HDL levels are not automatically interpreted as providing additional protection in every situation.

## Important Interpretation Factors

HDL should be evaluated together with LDL, total cholesterol and triglycerides.

Lifestyle factors such as physical activity, smoking and diet can influence HDL.

## Important Limitations

HDL is only one part of cardiovascular risk assessment.

A single HDL measurement cannot determine overall cardiovascular health.

## When Should the Result Be Discussed With a Healthcare Professional?

Discuss an unexpectedly low or otherwise abnormal HDL result with a qualified healthcare professional.

## Related Tests / Context

LDL cholesterol, total cholesterol, triglycerides and non-HDL cholesterol.
