# Triglycerides

## Test Name
Triglycerides

## Aliases
TG, Triglyceride Level, Serum Triglycerides

## Category
Lipid Profile

## What Does This Test Measure?

Triglycerides are a type of fat found in the blood.

The triglyceride test measures the amount of triglycerides in the blood.

## Why Is This Test Performed?

Triglycerides are commonly included in a lipid profile to assess cardiovascular risk and monitor treatment.

## What Can a Low Result Mean?

Low triglyceride levels are usually not a major concern by themselves.

They may occur in some nutritional or medical conditions.

## What Can a High Result Mean?

High triglycerides may be associated with increased cardiovascular risk.

Very high triglyceride levels can also increase the risk of pancreatitis.

Possible contributors include:

- Obesity
- Diabetes
- Certain medicines
- Excess alcohol intake
- Some genetic conditions
- Certain medical conditions

## Important Interpretation Factors

Triglyceride results can be affected by recent food intake, so preparation requirements should follow the laboratory's instructions.

Triglycerides should be interpreted together with the rest of the lipid profile.

## Important Limitations

Triglycerides alone cannot determine cardiovascular risk or the cause of an abnormal result.

## When Should the Result Be Discussed With a Healthcare Professional?

A persistently elevated triglyceride level should be discussed with a qualified healthcare professional, particularly if levels are very high or other cardiovascular risk factors are present.

## Related Tests / Context

Total cholesterol, LDL cholesterol, HDL cholesterol and non-HDL cholesterol.
