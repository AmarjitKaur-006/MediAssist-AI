# Total Cholesterol

## Test Name
Total Cholesterol

## Aliases
Total Cholesterol, TC, Cholesterol

## Category
Lipid Profile

## What Does This Test Measure?

Total cholesterol measures the overall amount of cholesterol in the blood.

Cholesterol is a waxy, fat-like substance that the body needs for normal functions but excessive amounts can contribute to plaque buildup in arteries.

## Why Is This Test Performed?

Total cholesterol is commonly included in a lipid profile.

It may be used to:

- Assess cardiovascular risk
- Monitor cholesterol levels
- Help evaluate the effect of lifestyle changes or treatment

## What Can a Low Result Mean?

A low total cholesterol result is generally interpreted in the context of the person's overall health.

Very low levels can occur in some medical conditions, nutritional problems, or with certain treatments.

## What Can a High Result Mean?

High total cholesterol may be associated with increased risk of cardiovascular disease.

However, total cholesterol alone does not show which types of cholesterol are contributing to the result.

## Important Interpretation Factors

Total cholesterol should be considered together with LDL, HDL and triglycerides.

Other cardiovascular risk factors such as age, blood pressure, smoking, diabetes and family history also influence overall risk.

## Important Limitations

Total cholesterol alone cannot determine a person's cardiovascular risk or diagnose a specific disease.

## When Should the Result Be Discussed With a Healthcare Professional?

An abnormal cholesterol result should be discussed with a qualified healthcare professional, especially when persistent or accompanied by other cardiovascular risk factors.

## Related Tests / Context

LDL cholesterol, HDL cholesterol, triglycerides and non-HDL cholesterol.
