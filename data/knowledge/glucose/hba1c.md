# Hemoglobin A1C (HbA1c)

## Test Name
Hemoglobin A1C

## Aliases
HbA1c, A1C, Glycated Hemoglobin, Glycosylated Hemoglobin

## Category
Blood Glucose / Diabetes

## What Does This Test Measure?

HbA1c measures the percentage of hemoglobin in red blood cells that has glucose attached to it.

It provides an estimate of average blood glucose over approximately the previous two to three months.

## Why Is This Test Performed?

HbA1c may be used to:

- Screen for prediabetes
- Diagnose diabetes
- Monitor blood glucose control in people with diabetes

It does not usually require fasting.

## What Can a Low Result Mean?

A lower HbA1c generally reflects lower average blood glucose.

An unusually low result may need context if it is unexpected or inconsistent with other measurements.

## What Can a High Result Mean?

A higher HbA1c indicates a higher average blood glucose level over the previous several months.

Elevated values may be associated with prediabetes or diabetes depending on the applicable diagnostic criteria.

## Important Interpretation Factors

HbA1c can be affected by conditions that change red blood cell lifespan or hemoglobin characteristics.

Examples can include certain anemias, blood loss, recent blood transfusion and some hemoglobin disorders.

## Important Limitations

HbA1c is not a direct measurement of current blood glucose.

Some medical conditions can make the result less reliable.

## When Should the Result Be Discussed With a Healthcare Professional?

An abnormal HbA1c result should be discussed with a qualified healthcare professional, particularly when it is persistently elevated or inconsistent with other glucose measurements.

## Related Tests / Context

Fasting glucose, random glucose and oral glucose tolerance testing.
