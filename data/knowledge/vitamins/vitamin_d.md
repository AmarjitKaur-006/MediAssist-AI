# Vitamin D

## Test Name
25-Hydroxy Vitamin D

## Aliases
Vitamin D, 25(OH)D, 25-Hydroxyvitamin D, Vitamin D3/D2 Total

## Category
Vitamins

## What Does This Test Measure?

The 25-hydroxy vitamin D blood test measures the main form of vitamin D circulating in the blood.

Vitamin D is important for bone health and also has roles in normal muscle, nerve and immune function.

## Why Is This Test Performed?

Vitamin D testing may be used to:

- Evaluate possible vitamin D deficiency
- Investigate bone-health problems
- Monitor people at risk of deficiency
- Monitor vitamin D treatment when clinically appropriate

## What Can a Low Result Mean?

A low vitamin D result may indicate inadequate vitamin D status.

Possible contributing factors include:

- Limited sunlight exposure
- Inadequate dietary intake
- Problems with intestinal absorption
- Certain medicines
- Certain medical conditions affecting vitamin D metabolism

## What Can a High Result Mean?

Very high vitamin D levels can occur with excessive supplementation.

Excessive vitamin D can cause abnormally high calcium levels and other health problems.

## Important Interpretation Factors

Vitamin D status should be interpreted using the laboratory's reference range and the patient's clinical context.

Sun exposure, diet, supplements, age, skin characteristics and medical conditions can influence vitamin D status.

## Important Limitations

A vitamin D result alone cannot explain the cause of a low or high value.

Interpretation should consider symptoms, medical history and other relevant laboratory results.

## When Should the Result Be Discussed With a Healthcare Professional?

An abnormal vitamin D result should be discussed with a qualified healthcare professional, especially when significantly abnormal or when supplementation is being considered.

## Related Tests / Context

Calcium, phosphorus, parathyroid hormone and other bone-health measurements when clinically appropriate.
