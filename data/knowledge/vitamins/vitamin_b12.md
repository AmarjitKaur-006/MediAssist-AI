# Vitamin B12

## Test Name
Vitamin B12

## Aliases
Vitamin B12 Test, Cobalamin, B12

## Category
Vitamins

## What Does This Test Measure?

The vitamin B12 test measures the amount of vitamin B12 in the blood.

Vitamin B12 is needed for normal red blood cell formation, nervous system function and DNA production.

## Why Is This Test Performed?

Vitamin B12 testing may be used when evaluating:

- Anemia
- Numbness or tingling
- Certain neurological symptoms
- Nutritional deficiencies
- Malabsorption conditions

It may also be used to monitor people at risk of vitamin B12 deficiency.

## What Can a Low Result Mean?

Low vitamin B12 may be associated with:

- Inadequate dietary intake
- Problems absorbing vitamin B12
- Certain gastrointestinal conditions
- Certain medicines
- Pernicious anemia

Long-term deficiency can affect blood-cell production and the nervous system.

## What Can a High Result Mean?

High vitamin B12 can occur for several reasons and does not necessarily indicate excessive dietary intake.

Possible associations include supplementation and certain liver, kidney or blood-related conditions.

The cause should be evaluated in context.

## Important Interpretation Factors

Vitamin B12 should be interpreted together with symptoms, medical history and other blood tests when appropriate.

Some people can have functional vitamin B12 problems even when the serum B12 value is not clearly low.

## Important Limitations

A serum B12 measurement alone cannot always determine whether tissue-level vitamin B12 availability is adequate.

Additional tests may sometimes be considered.

## When Should the Result Be Discussed With a Healthcare Professional?

An abnormal vitamin B12 result should be discussed with a qualified healthcare professional, particularly when neurological symptoms or anemia are present.

## Related Tests / Context

Folate, methylmalonic acid, homocysteine, CBC and hemoglobin.
