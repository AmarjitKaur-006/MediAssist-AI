# Thyroid-Stimulating Hormone (TSH)

## Test Name
Thyroid-Stimulating Hormone

## Aliases
TSH, Thyrotropin

## Category
Thyroid Function

## What Does This Test Measure?

TSH is a hormone produced by the pituitary gland that signals the thyroid gland to produce thyroid hormones.

The TSH blood test measures the amount of TSH in the blood.

## Why Is This Test Performed?

TSH testing may be used to:

- Screen for thyroid disorders
- Evaluate symptoms associated with abnormal thyroid function
- Help diagnose hypothyroidism or hyperthyroidism
- Monitor thyroid treatment

## What Can a Low Result Mean?

A low TSH may occur when the thyroid is producing too much thyroid hormone.

It can also occur in some pituitary disorders or as a result of certain medicines and clinical conditions.

## What Can a High Result Mean?

A high TSH may occur when the thyroid is not producing enough thyroid hormone.

It can also be affected by certain medicines, pituitary conditions or other clinical circumstances.

## Important Interpretation Factors

TSH should commonly be interpreted with thyroid hormone measurements such as free T4 and, when appropriate, T3.

Interpretation can vary with age, pregnancy, medicines and the laboratory's reference range.

## Important Limitations

TSH alone cannot determine the exact cause of an abnormal thyroid result.

A healthcare professional may need additional thyroid tests and clinical information.

## When Should the Result Be Discussed With a Healthcare Professional?

An abnormal TSH result should be discussed with a qualified healthcare professional, particularly when persistent or accompanied by symptoms.

## Related Tests / Context

Free T4, total T4, free T3 and total T3.
